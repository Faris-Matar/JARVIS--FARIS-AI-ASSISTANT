# Layout Pattern: Section Rhythm & Motion Budget

**What:** Rules of thumb for pacing a scroll-heavy page so the big moments
actually land. Spectacle needs silence around it.

## Rhythm

- Alternate **set-pieces** (pinned/scrubbed/3D moments) with **rest sections**
  (calm, mostly static, generous whitespace). Never two set-pieces adjacent.
- Budget: a one-page site supports **2–3 set-pieces max**. Each set-piece pin:
  100–300vh. Total page: usually 8–14 viewport-heights of scroll.
- The single biggest moment belongs at "the turn" (pain → dream pivot), not
  the hero. Heroes establish; turns transform.
- Last section before the CTA should be the calmest on the page — let the
  decision breathe.

## Scale & whitespace

- Display type earns its size from contrast: oversized headlines need small,
  quiet supporting text nearby.
- Edge-to-edge moments read stronger when the sections around them are
  contained (max-w-6xl-ish). Alternate container widths deliberately.
- Vertical padding is a design element: rest sections ≥ 30vh padding; cramped
  rest sections defeat their purpose.

## Motion grammar

- Pick ONE text-reveal language per site (line mask / char stagger /
  scramble) and ONE easing personality. Consistency is what makes motion feel
  designed rather than decorated.
- Scroll-scrubbed motion = narrative ("you control the story").
  Triggered one-shot motion = punctuation ("the site reacts to you").
  Use scrub for beats, one-shots for arrival moments.
- Everything that moves must also work frozen: design the static frame first,
  then choreograph how it assembles.

## Mobile

- Pins shorten or unpin; horizontal journeys stack vertically; 3D drops
  particle counts / swaps to a poster frame where needed. The story must
  survive with motion at 30% intensity.
