# Layout Pattern: Narrative Single Page

**What:** The page is structured as a story arc, not a stack of features. The
visitor is taken from their current pain, through the turn, to the dream
state, and lands at the action — section order follows emotional sequence,
not corporate convention.

**The arc:**

1. **Hook (hero)** — name the pain or the outcome in one line. The 3D
   centrepiece establishes the brand's world. One CTA visible.
2. **Pain mirror** — show them you understand their current reality. Often a
   pinned story sequence: 2–3 beats of escalating recognition.
3. **The turn** — the transformation moment. This is where the biggest scroll
   set-piece belongs (morph, camera move, build-itself reveal). The copy
   pivots from pain to possibility.
4. **The proof** — verified evidence only. Numbers, work, testimonials. Keep
   the motion calmer here; credibility likes stability.
5. **The path** — how it works, usually 3–4 steps. Horizontal journey or
   stepped pin both fit.
6. **The ask** — the CTA section. Repeat the dream state in one line, make
   the action frictionless. Footer carries the practical links.

**Why it works:** mirrors the persuasion sequence the copy already follows
(pain → dream → proof → action), so motion, copy, and layout reinforce each
other instead of competing.

**Variations:**
- Compress 2+3 into one long pinned sequence for short attention industries.
- For multi-audience brands, fork after the hook ("I need X" / "I need Y")
  into two mini-arcs that reconverge at proof.

**Don't:** insert generic sections (logo walls, stat counters, card grids)
between arc beats because "sites have them". Every section must be a beat in
the story or it's cut.
